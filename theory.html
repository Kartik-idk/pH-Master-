Understanding pH Theory

The pH of a solution is a measure of how acidic or basic (alkaline) it is. It is based on the concentration of hydrogen ions [H⁺] in the solution.

📌 What is pH?

pH = −log[H⁺]

The pH scale ranges from 0 to 14:

  • 0–3: Strong Acid
  • 4–6: Weak Acid
  • 7: Neutral
  • 8–11: Weak Base
  • 12–14: Strong Base

🧪 Strong Acids

[H⁺] = concentration of acid
  • HCl
  • HNO₃
  • H₂SO₄ (diprotic)

🧪 Weak Acids

[H⁺] = √(Ka × C)
  • CH₃COOH (Acetic Acid)
  • H₂CO₃ (Carbonic Acid)
  • HCOOH (Formic Acid)

🧪 Strong Bases

[OH⁻] = concentration of base
pH = 14 − pOH

🧪 Weak Bases

[OH⁻] = √(Kb × C)
  • NH₃ (Ammonia)
  • Pyridine

⚖ Diprotic Acids & Bases

Effective concentration = 2 × initial concentration
  • H₂SO₄ releases 2 H⁺
  • Ca(OH)₂ releases 2 OH⁻

📘 Why pH Matters

  • Chemical reactions
  • Biology and metabolism
  • Water quality testing
  • Food processing
  • Environmental science
